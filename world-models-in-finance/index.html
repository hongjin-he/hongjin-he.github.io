World Models in Finance: What They Are and How MicroWorld Builds One

By Hongjin He (HongJin HE, 何泓錦), Co-Founder & CTO of AlphaFlow. Updated 2026-10-21.

What is a world model in finance?

A world model of finance describes how a market evolves. It represents the state of the market (who holds what, at what leverage, with what liquidity), the agents that act on that state, and the rules that bind them, such as margin requirements and liquidation. Given a shock, it rolls the system forward and shows what happens next.

How is this different from a factor model or a backtest?

A factor model learns correlations between features and returns. A backtest replays one historical path. Neither can answer "what happens if this shock hits a market where participants react to one another?" A world model represents the mechanism, so it can simulate events that never appeared in the data. Factor models learn correlations; world models aim at causal structure.

Why start where every position is public?

On on-chain exchanges such as Hyperliquid, every account's positions, leverage and liquidation price are public. That makes it possible to reconstruct the state of the market and to check simulated behaviour against what actually happened. In traditional markets that state is hidden, so on-chain markets are a natural first testbed.

MicroWorld: a worked example

MicroWorld reconstructs real account states on Hyperliquid and simulates how a price shock cascades through forced liquidations. In one public snapshot (13 October 2026) the engine covered roughly 3,100 accounts and about $7.8B of notional. In an example shock, BTC falling 8% in 15 minutes under stressed liquidity, the market move of about 9.7% grew to about 11.7% once forced liquidations fed back. This is an illustration on literature-standard parameters, not a forecast.

The longer-term goal is for every participant to be played by an AI agent standing in for a real one, so that important financial decisions can be simulated before the market sees them. See the public showcase on GitHub and the AlphaFlow website. The engine itself is closed-source.
